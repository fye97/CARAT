\section{Experimental Protocols and Additional Results}
\label{sec:appendix-experiments}

This appendix records the exact scope, CARAT configuration, component ablation, client-population scalability study, and runtime diagnostic underlying Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Completed Empirical Scope}

The completed comparison covers CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, Dirichlet non-i.i.d.\ partitions, $10\%$ malicious clients, and five attacks: ALIE, FangAttack, MinMax, MinSum, and Mimic. The paper reports an unattacked Mean reference and attacked results for NormClipping, MultiKrum, FLTrust, FLDetector, and CARAT. The $10\%$ comparison selects $150$ CARAT runs across the two datasets, three heterogeneity levels, five attacks, and five seeds; the baseline defenses and clean Mean references also use five seeds. Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction} additionally uses the $10\%$, $20\%$, $30\%$, and $40\%$ runs of CARAT, the four baseline defenses, and undefended Mean for the same three heterogeneity levels and five attacks, totaling $3{,}600$ completed runs. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-scalability} compares CARAT with the four baseline defenses on CIFAR-100 with $20$, $40$, and $60$ clients over seeds $42$--$46$.

\subsection{Ablation Study of CARAT Components}
\label{sec:appendix-component-study}

We conduct a one-component-at-a-time ablation on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, $20\%$ malicious clients, non-i.i.d.\ $\alpha=0.5$, and $200$ communication rounds. This is a separate, higher-adversary experiment rather than a ratio-matched ablation of the main $10\%$ comparison. We report ALIE and FangAttack, each with the same five seeds $42$--$46$ for every variant. Full CARAT uses $T=8$ hidden tasks, MAD clipping, rank-penalty weight $0.20$, and temporal-prior weight $0.05$. Each reduced variant changes only one of these settings: one task sets $T=1$, no clipping disables MAD clipping, no rank penalty sets its weight to zero, and no temporal prior sets its weight to zero. The other recorded settings are held fixed within this study.

Full CARAT has the highest mean under both reported attacks: $46.81\%$ for ALIE and $47.59\%$ for FangAttack. Relative to the reduced variants, its gains range from $0.13$ to $0.55$ percentage points under ALIE and from $0.17$ to $0.80$ points under FangAttack. The differences are modest compared with the $0.59$--$1.18$-point seed-level standard deviations; in particular, one task nearly matches eight tasks under ALIE. These results support a limited benefit for the complete configuration in this higher-adversary setting, not a claim that every component is independently necessary or that the same ranking holds at $10\%$ malicious clients or on other datasets.

CARAT has the highest five-attack mean among the defenses for every heterogeneity level and client count. Its margin over the strongest baseline, FLDetector in every setting, is $2.74$--$3.97$ points. The two methods have similar clean accuracy (within $1.48$ points), but the five attacks reduce FLDetector by $2.82$--$4.31$ points and CARAT by $0.74$--$1.29$ points. NormClipping and FLTrust lose less than $1.2$ points under attack but remain $16$--$30$ points below CARAT even without attack, so their gap reflects lower clean accuracy rather than weaker filtering. MultiKrum benefits most from a larger federation: its five-attack mean rises from $37.83\%$ to $48.78\%$ at $\alpha=1.0$ and from $24.12\%$ to $37.40\%$ at $\alpha=0.1$ between $20$ and $60$ clients, but stays below FLDetector and CARAT.

For CARAT, the no-attack results improve as the client population grows, so increasing the number of clients does not by itself prevent optimization. Under attack, its five-attack mean increases from $20$ to $40$ to $60$ clients in every heterogeneity setting. At $60$ clients, it reaches $55.92\%$, $54.65\%$, and $48.45\%$ for $\alpha=1.0$, $0.5$, and $0.1$. No CARAT seed finishes at chance level for any attack, client count, or heterogeneity setting; the lowest single-run accuracy is $41.97\%$. These results support scaling to $60$ clients in the evaluated settings at $10\%$ malicious clients. As in the main comparison, attacks at this fraction barely degrade the defenses that keep clean accuracy, so the table tests scalability more than robustness.

\subsection{Runtime Diagnostic}
\label{sec:appendix-runtime}

Figure~\ref{fig:verification-curves} reports a separate, earlier CIFAR-100/i.i.d.\ ALIE verification run with three seeds. CARAT records an average per-round time of $9.94$ seconds, compared with $2.80$ seconds for Mean under attack, or approximately $3.6\times$ higher wall-clock cost. This premium is consistent with evaluating every candidate update on repeated hidden tasks. The diagnostic predates the five-seed sweep and is not hardware-normalized for the main comparison.
