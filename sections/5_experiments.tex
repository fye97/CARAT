\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate CIFAR-10/VGG19~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning,simonyan2015verydeep} and CIFAR-100/ResNet18~\citep{he2016deep} with $20$ fully participating clients, Dirichlet non-i.i.d.\ partitions ($\alpha\in\{1.0,0.5,0.1\}$), $200$ communication rounds, and four local epochs per round. We test ALIE~\citep{baruch2019alie}, FangAttack~\citep{fang2020local}, MinMax, MinSum~\citep{shejwalkar2021manipulating}, and Mimic~\citep{karimireddy2022bucketing} with $10\%$ malicious clients, and compare CARAT with NormClipping, MultiKrum~\citep{blanchard2017krum}, FLTrust~\citep{cao2021fltrust}, and FLDetector~\citep{zhang2022fldetector}. Unattacked Mean is the clean reference. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-experiments} gives the optimizer and method details.

Figures~\ref{fig:table1-curves-cifar100} and~\ref{fig:table1-curves-cifar10} report means and sample-standard-deviation bands over seeds $42$--$46$; numerical comparisons below use round 200. CARAT uses $T=8$ hidden tasks and a $512$-example server pool, whereas FLTrust uses $100$ server examples. Both pools are drawn from the training set without removing those examples from client partitions, so the curves compare the recorded method--reference configurations rather than isolated aggregation rules. The shared NoAttack + Mean curve is excluded from defense rankings.

\subsection{Experimental Results}

\subsubsection{Robustness to Model-Poisoning Attacks}
Figure~\ref{fig:table1-curves-cifar100} shows that CARAT closely tracks the clean trajectory after the learning-rate transitions, rather than reaching a similar endpoint through a late recovery. On CIFAR-100 with $\alpha\in\{1.0,0.5\}$, CARAT is the strongest attacked defense under FangAttack and is second under each of the other conventional attacks. In those six second-place cases it trails the strongest defense by only $0.18$--$0.54$ percentage points. It also avoids FLDetector's sharp FangAttack degradation and exceeds MultiKrum and FLTrust throughout both settings. Relative to the NoAttack + Mean references of $52.47\%$ and $51.61\%$, CARAT's conventional-attack results remain within $0.95$--$1.91$ points.

At $\alpha=0.1$, CARAT is the strongest attacked defense under FangAttack and MinMax, and is second under ALIE and MinSum by $0.29$ and $0.16$ points, respectively. Its four means remain within $1.14$--$2.85$ points of the $45.94\%$ clean reference, with seed-level standard deviations below one percentage point. The lower attacker fraction therefore removes the chance-level collapses observed in the archived $20\%$ CARAT runs. This is evidence of stability for the evaluated $10\%$ threat level, not evidence that the same behavior persists as the malicious population grows.

On CIFAR-10 at $\alpha=1.0$, CARAT is the strongest attacked defense under FangAttack and is second under ALIE, MinMax, and MinSum, with gaps of $0.49$, $0.44$, and $0.53$ points. At $\alpha=0.5$, it leads ALIE, FangAttack, and MinMax, but one MinSum seed finishes at chance-level $10\%$ accuracy; the resulting round-200 accuracy of $63.58\pm29.96\%$ is second to FLDetector and lies $13.49$ points below the $77.07\%$ clean reference. At $\alpha=0.1$, CARAT leads ALIE and FangAttack, while one MinMax seed and one MinSum seed again finish at chance level, producing $47.65\pm21.10\%$ and $43.94\pm19.28\%$. The widening bands in Figure~\ref{fig:table1-curves-cifar10} show when this seed sensitivity emerges instead of only exposing it at round 200.

Mimic copies a fixed benign update rather than optimizing an outlier. CARAT ranks second on CIFAR-100 at all three heterogeneity levels, trailing FLDetector by $0.46$--$1.08$ points. On CIFAR-10, it is second at $\alpha=1.0$ and leads at $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.1\}$ with $76.96\%$ and $53.96\%$. It exceeds NormClipping, MultiKrum, and FLTrust in every Mimic setting, indicating that hidden-task validation retains signal against copied benign updates.

\subsubsection{Impact of Client Heterogeneity}
On CIFAR-100, CARAT's accuracy averaged across all five attacks is $51.30\%$, $49.94\%$, and $44.05\%$ for $\alpha=1.0$, $0.5$, and $0.1$, respectively, trailing the corresponding clean references by $1.17$, $1.67$, and $1.89$ points. The $1.36$-point decline from $\alpha=1.0$ to $0.5$ grows to $5.89$ points from $0.5$ to $0.1$, but remains consistent across attacks rather than being driven by isolated seed collapses. On CIFAR-10, the corresponding attack averages are $79.38\%$, $74.13\%$, and $50.81\%$, compared with clean references of $79.83\%$, $77.07\%$, and $53.53\%$. The larger cross-setting drops of $5.25$ and $23.32$ points reflect both stronger heterogeneity and the MinSum/MinMax failures. Client heterogeneity therefore remains an important source of degradation even when the malicious-client fraction is $10\%$, with a substantially sharper effect on CIFAR-10/VGG19.

\subsubsection{Impact of Attacker Fraction}
We next vary the malicious-client fraction from $10\%$ to $40\%$ (two to eight of the same $20$ clients) while retaining the three Dirichlet settings and five attacks. Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction} averages the five attack accuracies within each seed, then reports the mean and sample standard deviation across seeds at round 200. On CIFAR-100, the $10\%$-to-$40\%$ increase reduces CARAT's attack-averaged accuracy from $51.30\%$ to $42.17\%$ at $\alpha=1.0$, from $49.94\%$ to $34.09\%$ at $\alpha=0.5$, and from $44.05\%$ to $26.42\%$ at $\alpha=0.1$. CIFAR-10 shows the same endpoint trend, declining from $79.38\%$ to $62.55\%$, $74.13\%$ to $56.60\%$, and $50.81\%$ to $28.15\%$, respectively. The trend is not strictly monotone: the CIFAR-10 $\alpha=0.5$ mean improves slightly at $20\%$ because a MinSum seed fails at $10\%$. Higher attacker fractions generally magnify the impact of heterogeneity, but the plot summarizes attack-specific outcomes and does not imply uniform degradation for every attack. CARAT's weight cap uses the specified attacker fraction, so this sweep changes both the malicious population and the supplied prior; it does not test prior misspecification.

\subsubsection{Scope of the Evidence}
The main comparison does not cover partial participation, ratio-matched larger-federation baselines, backdoor or data-poisoning attacks, misspecified attacker fractions, or adaptive attackers that infer the hidden tasks. The NoAttack + Mean curve is a clean reference rather than an attacked defense, and the unequal CARAT/FLTrust reference budgets further limit comparison. Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction} extends only CARAT to higher attacker fractions, not the baseline comparisons. The separate component study, CARAT-only client-population diagnostic, and earlier three-seed runtime diagnostic are reported in Appendices~\ref{sec:appendix-component-study}, \ref{sec:appendix-scalability}, and~\ref{sec:appendix-runtime}, respectively. Together with the CIFAR-10 seed-level failures, these boundaries restrict the claim to the evaluated settings.
