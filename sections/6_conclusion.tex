\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CARAT validates candidate updates through hidden, class-balanced task certificates rather than relying only on geometric centrality or a single trusted reference direction. In the five-seed CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 comparison with $10\%$ malicious clients, CARAT achieves the highest defensive accuracy in $12$ of the $30$ attacked settings and the second-highest in $17$. CIFAR-100 remains stable across seeds at that ratio, while CIFAR-10 exposes isolated chance-level failures under MinSum and MinMax as heterogeneity increases. A CARAT-only sweep shows decreasing attack-averaged accuracy as the malicious fraction rises to $40\%$. These observations support conditional competitiveness rather than universal robustness. Disjoint reference data, matched auxiliary-data budgets, ratio-matched baseline comparisons, prior misspecification, and adaptive attacks remain important next steps.
