\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CARAT validates candidate updates through hidden, class-balanced task certificates rather than relying only on geometric centrality or a single trusted reference direction. In the five-seed CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 comparison with $10\%$ malicious clients, CARAT achieves the highest defensive accuracy in $15$ of the $30$ attacked settings and the second-highest in $15$. No CARAT seed in the main comparison finishes at chance level, although genuine robustness failures remain at higher malicious-client fractions, particularly under ALIE and FangAttack on CIFAR-10 at $\alpha=0.1$. As the malicious fraction rises to $40\%$, CARAT's attack-averaged accuracy decreases but stays above every baseline except FLTrust on CIFAR-10 at $\alpha=0.1$ with $30$--$40\%$ malicious clients; at $10$--$20\%$, undefended averaging is nearly as accurate, so the advantage appears mainly at higher fractions. These observations support conditional competitiveness rather than universal robustness. A held-out, budget-matched reference pool leaves CARAT's accuracy unchanged. Prior misspecification, certificate-aware adaptive attacks, and direct evidence for class-local filtering remain important next steps.
