\section{Experimental Protocols and Additional Results}
\label{sec:appendix-experiments}

This appendix records the exact scope, CARAT configuration, component ablation, client-population scalability study, and runtime diagnostic underlying Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Completed Empirical Scope}

The completed comparison covers CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, Dirichlet non-i.i.d.\ partitions, $10\%$ malicious clients, and five attacks: ALIE, FangAttack, MinMax, MinSum, and Mimic. The paper reports an unattacked Mean reference and attacked results for NormClipping, MultiKrum, FLTrust, SignGuard (not under Mimic), FLDetector, and CARAT. The $10\%$ comparison selects $150$ CARAT runs across the two datasets, three heterogeneity levels, five attacks, and five seeds; the baseline defenses and clean Mean references also use five seeds. Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction} additionally uses the $10\%$, $20\%$, $30\%$, and $40\%$ runs of CARAT, the four baseline defenses, and undefended Mean for the same three heterogeneity levels and five attacks, totaling $3{,}600$ completed runs. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-scalability} compares CARAT with the four baseline defenses on CIFAR-100 with $20$, $40$, and $60$ clients over seeds $42$--$46$. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-reference-control} adds a held-out, budget-matched reference-data control with Zeno, the measured malicious weight, and a SignGuard comparison at $10\%$ and $20\%$ malicious clients.

\subsection{Ablation Study of CARAT Components}
\label{sec:appendix-component-study}

We conduct a one-component-at-a-time ablation on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, $20\%$ malicious clients, non-i.i.d.\ $\alpha=0.5$, and $200$ communication rounds. This is a separate, higher-adversary experiment rather than a ratio-matched ablation of the main $10\%$ comparison. We report ALIE and FangAttack, each with the same five seeds $42$--$46$ for every variant. Full CARAT uses $T=8$ hidden tasks, MAD clipping, rank-penalty weight $0.20$, and temporal-prior weight $0.05$. Each reduced variant changes only one of these settings: one task sets $T=1$, no clipping disables MAD clipping, no rank penalty sets its weight to zero, and no temporal prior sets its weight to zero. Full CARAT has the highest mean under both reported attacks: $46.81\%$ for ALIE and $47.59\%$ for FangAttack. Relative to the reduced variants, its gains range from $0.13$ to $0.55$ percentage points under ALIE and from $0.17$ to $0.80$ points under FangAttack. \subsection{Reference-Data Control, Zeno, and Malicious Weight}
\label{sec:appendix-reference-control}

In the main comparison, CARAT's $512$-example pool and FLTrust's $100$-example set are drawn from the training set without removing those examples from client partitions, so auxiliary-data access differs across methods. To separate the aggregation rule from this access, we remove a class-balanced pool of $512$ training examples, fixed per seed, from every client partition. FLTrust, Zeno~\citep{xie2019zeno}, and CARAT then draw server data only from this held-out pool, each with $100$ and with $512$ examples; for a given seed, all three methods use the same $100$-example subset. The study uses CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ clients, $10\%$ malicious clients, non-i.i.d.\ $\alpha=0.5$, the five attacks of the main comparison, seeds $42$--$46$, and default method parameters ($300$ runs). Zeno scores each update on a $64$-example mini-batch drawn from its server data every round, uses magnitude penalty $\rho=5\times10^{-4}$, and discards as many lowest-scoring updates as there are malicious clients. Removing the pool changes the data being partitioned, so the client partitions are redrawn with the same Dirichlet parameter; the comparison with the original in-training configuration therefore changes both the overlap and the partition, whereas the $100$- and $512$-example runs share the same held-out partition. With $100$ CIFAR-100 examples the pool holds one example per class, so all $T=8$ tasks reuse the same $100$ examples.

Table~\ref{tab:malicious-weight} reports the malicious weight $q_{\mathcal{M}}$ that Proposition~\ref{prop:malicious-mass-bound} takes as given. At $10\%$ malicious clients, CARAT reduces $q_{\mathcal{M}}$ only under FangAttack; the near-mean attacks ALIE, MinMax, and MinSum keep the capped weight, as they do under Zeno. These attacks are constructed to imitate benign update statistics; Proposition~\ref{prop:malicious-mass-bound} bounds their influence through the cap and clipping, and empirically each of these three attacks lowers CARAT's round-200 accuracy by at most $1.6$ points relative to its no-attack accuracy in the main comparison.

Down-weighting appears as attacks become more aggressive. In the attacker-fraction sweep (Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction}), CARAT logs per-round weights for seed $42$. Under FangAttack with $20$--$40\%$ malicious clients, $q_{\mathcal{M}}$ averages at most $0.05$ over the last $50$ rounds in $17$ of the $18$ dataset--$\alpha$--fraction settings and is zero in most of them; the exception is CIFAR-100 at $\alpha=0.5$ with $40\%$ malicious clients ($0.19$). In these runs the FangAttack clients' rank penalties exceed those of benign clients by $2.8$--$17$ robust (MAD) units at $30$--$40\%$. Under ALIE with $40\%$ malicious clients, whose perturbation grows with the attacker count, $q_{\mathcal{M}}$ averages at most $0.06$ over the first $20$ rounds in all six dataset--$\alpha$ settings and is zero throughout rounds $0$--$99$ at $\alpha=0.5$; in the same setting, undefended Mean reaches $24.68\%$ on CIFAR-10 at $\alpha=1.0$, whereas CARAT reaches $72.05\%$. Under MinMax and MinSum, the malicious clients keep the capped weight at every fraction, and CARAT remains within $3$ points of undefended averaging. Mimic, which replays a benign update, is the hardest case: at $30$--$40\%$ CARAT does not down-weight it and is $1.8$--$14.0$ points below undefended Mean. These per-round observations come from a single seed and describe the full rule.

CARAT has the highest five-attack mean among the defenses for every heterogeneity level and client count. Its margin over the strongest baseline, FLDetector in every setting, is $2.74$--$3.97$ points. The two methods have similar clean accuracy (within $1.48$ points), but the five attacks reduce FLDetector by $2.82$--$4.31$ points and CARAT by $0.74$--$1.29$ points. NormClipping and FLTrust lose less than $1.2$ points under attack but remain $16$--$30$ points below CARAT even without attack, so their gap reflects lower clean accuracy rather than weaker filtering. MultiKrum benefits most from a larger federation: its five-attack mean rises from $37.83\%$ to $48.78\%$ at $\alpha=1.0$ and from $24.12\%$ to $37.40\%$ at $\alpha=0.1$ between $20$ and $60$ clients, but stays below FLDetector and CARAT.

For CARAT, the no-attack results improve as the client population grows, so increasing the number of clients does not by itself prevent optimization. Under attack, its five-attack mean increases from $20$ to $40$ to $60$ clients in every heterogeneity setting. At $60$ clients, it reaches $55.92\%$, $54.65\%$, and $48.45\%$ for $\alpha=1.0$, $0.5$, and $0.1$. No CARAT seed finishes at chance level for any attack, client count, or heterogeneity setting; the lowest single-run accuracy is $41.97\%$. These results support scaling to $60$ clients in the evaluated settings at $10\%$ malicious clients. As in the main comparison, attacks at this fraction barely degrade the defenses that keep clean accuracy, so the table tests scalability more than robustness.
