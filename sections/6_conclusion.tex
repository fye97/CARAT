\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CARAT validates candidate updates through hidden, class-balanced task certificates rather than relying only on geometric centrality or a single trusted reference direction. In the five-seed CIFAR-100/ResNet18 evaluation with $10\%$ malicious clients, CARAT attains an average rank of $2.0$--$2.5$ under the four conventional attacks and ranks second among defenses with Mimic results across all three heterogeneity settings. Performance declines as heterogeneity increases, but all CARAT attack cells remain stable across seeds at this attacker fraction. Together with the strong unprotected Mean results on the conventional attacks and the small gaps in the separate $20\%$ component study, this evidence supports CARAT as a competitive reference-assisted mechanism within the evaluated threat level. Disjoint reference data, matched auxiliary-data budgets, adaptive attacks, and systematic evaluation at larger malicious-client fractions are important next steps.
