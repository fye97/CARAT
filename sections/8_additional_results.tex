\section{Experimental Protocols and Additional Results}
\label{sec:appendix-experiments}

This appendix records the exact scope, CARAT configuration, component ablation, client-population scalability study, and runtime diagnostic underlying Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Completed Empirical Scope}

The completed comparison covers CIFAR-10/VGG19 and CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, Dirichlet non-i.i.d.\ partitions, $10\%$ malicious clients, and five attacks: ALIE, FangAttack, MinMax, MinSum, and Mimic. The paper reports an unattacked Mean reference and attacked results for NormClipping, MultiKrum, FLTrust, FLDetector, and CARAT. The $10\%$ comparison selects $150$ CARAT runs across the two datasets, three heterogeneity levels, five attacks, and five seeds; the baseline defenses and clean Mean references also use five seeds. Figure~\ref{fig:attacker-fraction} additionally uses the $10\%$, $20\%$, $30\%$, and $40\%$ CARAT runs for the same three heterogeneity levels and five attacks, totaling $600$ completed runs. Baseline defenses are not compared across these higher fractions. Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-scalability} gives a CARAT-only comparison with $20$, $40$, and $60$ clients using the common completed seeds.

\subsection{Ablation Study of CARAT Components}
\label{sec:appendix-component-study}

We conduct a one-component-at-a-time ablation on CIFAR-100/ResNet18 with $20$ fully participating clients, $20\%$ malicious clients, non-i.i.d.\ $\alpha=0.5$, and $200$ communication rounds. This is a separate, higher-adversary experiment rather than a ratio-matched ablation of the main $10\%$ comparison. We report ALIE and FangAttack, each with the same five seeds $42$--$46$ for every variant. Full CARAT uses $T=8$ hidden tasks, MAD clipping, rank-penalty weight $0.20$, and temporal-prior weight $0.05$. Each reduced variant changes only one of these settings: one task sets $T=1$, no clipping disables MAD clipping, no rank penalty sets its weight to zero, and no temporal prior sets its weight to zero. The other recorded settings are held fixed within this study.

Full CARAT has the highest mean under both reported attacks: $46.81\%$ for ALIE and $47.59\%$ for FangAttack. Relative to the reduced variants, its gains range from $0.13$ to $0.55$ percentage points under ALIE and from $0.17$ to $0.80$ points under FangAttack. The differences are modest compared with the $0.59$--$1.18$-point seed-level standard deviations; in particular, one task nearly matches eight tasks under ALIE. These results support a limited benefit for the complete configuration in this higher-adversary setting, not a claim that every component is independently necessary or that the same ranking holds at $10\%$ malicious clients or on other datasets.

Because the available $60$-client archive is incomplete for CIFAR-100 at $\alpha=1.0$ beyond the first three seeds, Table~\ref{tab:carat-client-scaling} uses the common completed seeds $42$--$44$ for every client count and setting. The selected slice contains $324$ completed 200-round runs. For each seed, the attacked summary first averages ALIE, FangAttack, MinMax, MinSum, and Mimic; the table then reports the mean and sample standard deviation of these seed-level averages. The no-attack column runs CARAT without malicious updates and is distinct from the NoAttack + Mean reference in the main comparison.

The no-attack results improve or remain stable as the client population grows, so increasing the number of clients does not by itself prevent optimization. Under attack, CIFAR-10 also improves from $20$ to $40$ clients in all three heterogeneity regimes and remains strong at $60$ clients for $\alpha\in\{1.0,0.5\}$. The $\alpha=0.1$ five-attack mean decreases from $57.77\%$ at $40$ clients to $55.29\%$ at $60$, with high variability caused by two chance-level MinSum seeds.

CIFAR-100 exposes a different scaling failure. FangAttack finishes at chance-level $1\%$ accuracy for all three common seeds at $60$ clients and every reported $\alpha$, which lowers the five-attack averages despite improving no-attack accuracy. At $\alpha=0.1$, MinSum additionally collapses for one of three seeds at $40$ clients and two of three seeds at $60$ clients. Thus, these results show that CARAT's benign training behavior scales to $60$ clients, but its robustness does not scale uniformly across attacks. They are a CARAT-only diagnostic based on three common seeds, not a larger-federation baseline comparison or a complete five-seed result.

\subsection{Runtime Diagnostic}
\label{sec:appendix-runtime}

Figure~\ref{fig:verification-curves} reports a separate, earlier CIFAR-100/i.i.d.\ ALIE verification run with three seeds. CARAT records an average per-round time of $9.94$ seconds, compared with $2.80$ seconds for Mean under attack, or approximately $3.6\times$ higher wall-clock cost. This premium is consistent with evaluating every candidate update on repeated hidden tasks. The diagnostic predates the five-seed sweep and is not hardware-normalized for the main comparison.
